Create an eight-slide, typography-only Instagram carousel for **CUTTHROAT DISCIPLINE**, a premium fitness, accountability, and personal-discipline brand.

## Format

- Vertical Instagram carousel
- Exact dimensions: **1080 × 1350 pixels**
- Exact aspect ratio: **4:5**
- Eight separate slides
- Design every slide individually at 1080 × 1350
- Do not create a square canvas
- Do not place all slides on one landscape contact sheet
- Maintain consistent margins, alignment, branding, and typography across all eight slides

## Core message

The carousel contrasts people who repeatedly announce what they intend to do with people who quietly begin and execute.

It should confront people who constantly say:

- “I’ll start tomorrow.”
- “I’ll start Monday.”
- “I’m waiting for the right time.”
- “I need more motivation.”

The writing should feel sharp, uncomfortable, and hard-hitting. It should challenge procrastination and empty talk without attacking anyone’s appearance, intelligence, or value as a person.

The central idea is:

**Talking creates the feeling of progress. Action creates the evidence.**

## Visual direction

Create a premium, aggressive editorial design built entirely from typography and simple graphic elements.

The visual style should feel like:

- A high-end athletic campaign
- Luxury sports editorial design
- Modern magazine typography
- Confident, disciplined, and confrontational
- Clean enough to feel expensive
- Loud enough to stop someone from scrolling

Do not make the design resemble a generic motivational template.

Use generous negative space with purposeful typography. The words should dominate each slide, but they must not feel stretched, distorted, overcrowded, or forced against the edges.

The composition should feel controlled rather than chaotic.

## Typography

Use a bold, condensed headline font with the visual character of **Heading Now 31–38 Bold**.

If Heading Now is unavailable, use **Barlow Condensed ExtraBold** as the substitute.

Use **Gotham** for any small supporting text.

If Gotham is unavailable, use **Montserrat Medium or Bold** as the substitute.

Typography requirements:

- Headlines should be large and immediately readable
- Use natural letter proportions
- Never horizontally stretch or compress the font
- Never force every line to have the same width
- Allow short words to remain naturally shorter
- Create hierarchy through size, weight, color, position, and spacing
- Keep line breaks intentional
- Use tight but readable headline leading
- Maintain consistent safe margins
- Avoid placing important text too close to the edges
- Do not use tiny explanatory paragraphs
- Limit each slide to one primary idea
- Supporting copy should be used sparingly

The typography should occupy approximately 65–80% of the usable canvas while preserving enough breathing room to feel premium.

## Brand palette

Use a restrained CUTTHROAT DISCIPLINE palette:

- Obsidian black: `#090A0C`
- Charcoal black: `#15171B`
- Warm off-white: `#F3EFE6`
- Titanium silver: `#AEB3BA`
- Metallic gold: `#D6A928`
- Gold highlight: `#F1CF63`
- Deep crimson accent: `#8D1721`

Gold should identify the most important word or phrase on each slide.

Use crimson only as a restrained secondary accent, such as:

- A thin strike
- A small underline
- A narrow vertical rule
- A subtle registration mark

Do not make crimson the dominant color.

## Logo and footer

Use the supplied official gold-and-silver circular **CUTTHROAT DISCIPLINE** logo on every slide.

Logo requirements:

- Use the exact supplied logo
- Do not redraw it
- Do not replace it with initials
- Do not modify its colors
- Keep it small and consistent
- Position it near the upper-left corner
- Leave adequate space around it

Place the slide number near the upper-right corner:

**01 / 08**

Use this exact footer on every slide:

**DISCIPLINE - ACCOUNTABILITY - CONSISTENCY**

The footer should be small, clean, centered, and readable.

## Graphic elements

Use only restrained editorial graphic elements:

- Fine metallic-gold rules
- Thin silver borders
- Small crimson accents
- Oversized low-opacity slide numbers
- Subtle grain or paper texture
- Simple geometric frames
- Cropped lines and registration marks
- Controlled asymmetric alignment

These elements should reinforce the typography without competing with it.

Do not use:

- Photographs
- AI-generated people
- Athletes
- Human silhouettes
- Body parts
- Fitness equipment
- Stock imagery
- 3D characters
- Flames
- Lightning
- Excessive neon
- Glowing effects
- Emojis
- Cartoon graphics
- Cheap grunge effects
- Busy backgrounds
- Excessive shadows
- Random decorative shapes

## Slide copy and direction

### Slide 1 — Hook

**YOU KEEP SAYING
TOMORROW.**

**HOW MANY
TOMORROWS
DO YOU NEED?**

Make “TOMORROW” and “HOW MANY” metallic gold.

This should feel confrontational and immediately recognizable. Use a strong left-aligned composition with generous negative space around the question.

### Slide 2 — False progress

**TALKING ABOUT IT
MAKES YOU FEEL
PRODUCTIVE.**

**IT ISN’T
PROGRESS.**

Emphasize “IT ISN’T PROGRESS” in gold.

Use a clear visual break between the first statement and the correction. The second statement should land like a verdict.

### Slide 3 — The contrast

Divide the slide into two controlled typographic sections.

Upper section:

**TALKERS
ANNOUNCE.**

Lower section:

**DOERS
EXECUTE.**

Use off-white for “TALKERS ANNOUNCE” and metallic gold for “DOERS EXECUTE.”

The lower section should feel heavier and more decisive. Do not use a literal split-screen photograph.

### Slide 4 — The excuse

**“I’LL START
TOMORROW”**

is usually another way of saying:

**“I DON’T WANT
TO BE UNCOMFORTABLE
TODAY.”**

Use quotation marks as a strong editorial device.

Keep the first quotation in silver or off-white. Make the second quotation larger and more aggressive, with “UNCOMFORTABLE TODAY” in gold.

### Slide 5 — Accountability

**YOUR GOAL
ISN’T CONFUSED.**

**YOU KNOW
WHAT TO DO.**

**YOU KEEP
AVOIDING IT.**

Highlight “AVOIDING IT” in gold, with a restrained crimson underline or strike nearby.

This slide should feel direct and personal.

### Slide 6 — Intention versus evidence

**WANTING IT
EARNS YOU
NOTHING.**

**COMPLETED WORK
IS THE RECEIPT.**

Use “NOTHING” and “THE RECEIPT” in metallic gold.

Create a clean visual gap between desire and evidence. The typography should communicate that action is measurable.

### Slide 7 — Quiet execution

**STOP
ANNOUNCING
THE PLAN.**

**BUILD THE
EVIDENCE.**

Use off-white for the first statement and gold for “BUILD THE EVIDENCE.”

Keep this slide especially minimal. A thin gold rule or a few subtle stacked rectangles may suggest accumulated work.

### Slide 8 — Immediate action

**CLOSE
THIS POST.**

**DO ONE THING
RIGHT NOW.**

At the bottom, include three small but readable options:

**10 SQUATS**
**10-MINUTE WALK**
**PREP ONE MEAL**

End with:

**PICK ONE. FINISH IT.**

Make “RIGHT NOW” and “FINISH IT” metallic gold.

This slide should function as a command, not a motivational suggestion.

## Composition rules

- Maintain a minimum safe margin of approximately 60–75 pixels
- Keep every headline completely inside the canvas
- Do not crop letters accidentally
- Do not allow typography to collide with the logo or footer
- Do not stretch words merely to fill the width
- Do not make every line identical in length
- Alternate alignment only when it improves the message
- Keep the visual rhythm consistent across the full carousel
- Give the gold emphasis words room to breathe
- Make each slide understandable within one second
- Design for mobile-screen readability
- Ensure the carousel feels like one unified campaign

## Final quality standard

The result should look intentionally designed by a premium sports-brand art director.

It should feel:

- Severe
- Minimal
- Expensive
- Controlled
- Confrontational
- Highly readable
- Consistent
- Original

The final carousel must communicate that procrastination is a practiced behavior and that execution begins with one immediate, completed action.
